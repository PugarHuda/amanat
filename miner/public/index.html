How a claim settles

Four steps, and a person appears in none of them. The contract is the customer of the intelligence, not a screen someone reads it on.

  1. contract

    Opens a job

    It calls createJob against STORM_ALERT — the intent, not a miner. Which miner answers is the protocol's decision, and it goes to whoever currently ranks best.

  2. network

    Answers and is checked

    A miner returns a reading. Validators score it against ground truth and finalise it before anything is paid.

  3. protocol

    Delivers it back

    The result arrives at the contract through subnetMessage. It arrives packed the way that miner declares its fields, so the contract reads defensively and declines a payload it cannot interpret rather than guessing at a payout.

  4. contract

    Pays, or does not

    Risk at or above 0.75 pays the holder in the same transaction. Below it, the policy closes as declined. If no answer ever comes, the holder reclaims the reserve after 24 hours.

Call it from your own agent

Four ways in, cheapest first. The reading is useful anywhere a decision waits on weather — the contract is only one thing to do with it.

  1. free

    Straight over HTTP

    POST /forecast with lat, lon and an hours offset. No wallet, no key, no sign-up. Nothing is verified by validators on this route, and it costs nothing — those go together.

  2. $0.01

    From an agent, over MCP

    The Telegraph MCP server discovers every registered miner, so Amanat is already a tool in Claude Desktop, Cursor, Continue, Goose and LangChain — nothing to integrate. It appears as tg_amanat_weather_risk_forecast.

  3. $0.01

    Through the Engine

    Ask in plain language and the protocol picks the miner. You may or may not get Amanat — that is the point, and it is how you find out whether we are any good.

  4. $1.00

    From a contract

    Escrow USDC, open a job against an intent, receive the answer through subnetMessage. The contract on this page is a working receiver you can copy.
